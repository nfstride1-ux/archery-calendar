What is the Archery Asia Cup? Bailey Wildman's double gold headlines Stage 3 in Hangzhou

Sunday 22 November 2026

← All newsletters

Hi archers,

You might have seen "Asia Cup" mentioned around archery circles this year and wondered how it's different from the Hyundai Archery World Cup. It's World Archery Asia's own continental circuit: a series of World Ranking Tournaments run for and mostly by archers from Asian member federations, separate from World Archery's global World Cup series. The 2026 season has had three stages so far: Bangkok, Thailand in March; Sulaymaniyah, Iraq, which was postponed; and Hangzhou, China, which has just wrapped up.

STAGE 1: BANGKOK, THAILAND (MARCH)

Two bronzes confirmed for India, two more teams reached the final

On day two of Stage 1 in Bangkok, India's men's compound team (Rajat Chauhan, Rishabh Yadav and Uday Kamboj) topped qualification, sweeping the top four individual spots, but lost a tight semi-final 233-234 to Vietnam after squandering a five-point lead. They came back to beat Bhutan 234-232 in the bronze medal match. India's women's recurve team (Ruma Biswas, Kirti and Ridhi Phor) beat Malaysia 5-1 (sets 59-56, 53-50, 52-52) for bronze, having missed the podium at this venue the previous edition.

Two more Indian teams reached their finals against third-seeded Kazakhstan: the top-seeded men's recurve team (Devaang Gupta, Sukhchain Singh and Juyel Sarkar), who beat Malaysia 5-1 without dropping a set, and the women's compound team (Chikitha Taniparthi, Raj Kaur and Tejal Salve), who beat the Thai hosts 229-226 in a close semi-final. We don't have the result of either final, so we can't say yet whether those became gold or silver for India.

STAGE 2: SULAYMANIYAH, IRAQ — POSTPONED

Stage 2 was originally due in Sulaymaniyah, Iraq from 21 to 26 April, but World Archery pushed it back in light of the geopolitical situation in West Asia at the time, moving it to 25 to 30 October instead.

STAGE 3: HANGZHOU, CHINA — COMPLETE

17 to 21 November, 168 archers from 17 nations, India fielding a 16-member squad

Stage 3 wrapped up in Hangzhou on the Saturday, and the headline belongs to Australia's Bailey Wildman, who had the kind of finals day most archers only dream about: gold in the compound team, then straight back out for gold in the individual too. India had its best stage of the season either side of him — two bronzes locked away early in the week, then two more silvers in matches that came right down to the last arrow. Here's the full account:

  • Bailey Wildman (Australia) — DOUBLE GOLD. Wildman anchored Australia's compound team (with Jonathon Milne and Nathan Rowley) to a 234-229 win over India in the team final, then backed it up in the individual gold medal match, beating India's Ganesh Mani Ratnam Thirumuru 148-146. Two finals, two golds, same day.
  • Men's compound team — SILVER for India. Ganesh Mani Ratnam Thirumuru, Mohit and Manav beat Chinese Taipei 234-230 in the quarter-final and Singapore 231-224 in the semi-final to reach the gold medal match, where Wildman's Australia held firm 234-229 — a five-point margin across 24 arrows, close enough to sting.
  • Women's compound team — BRONZE for India. Prithika Pradeep, Tejal and Aditi beat Australia 232-225 in the quarter-final, lost the semi-final 227-234 to South Korea, then beat Indonesia 230-228 in the bronze match.
  • Men's recurve team — BRONZE for India. Ujjwal, Goldi and Abhyuday beat Indonesia 5-3 in the quarter-final, lost the semi-final 0-6 to Australia, then won the bronze match in a shoot-off against Chinese Taipei, 5(30)-4(28).
  • Women's recurve team — 4TH for India. Kirti, Yuktha Sri and Khushi beat North Korea 6-0 in the quarter-final, lost the semi-final to China 4(24)-5(27), then lost the bronze match 3-5 to South Korea.
  • Aditi Gopichand Swami, individual compound — SILVER for India. The 2023 Berlin world champion beat South Korea's Oh Yoohyun 143-142 in the semi-final to reach the gold medal match, then ran into China's Hu Jingwen on the kind of form that wins tournaments — Hu had already put out Prithika Pradeep 149-145 earlier in the day. Swami pushed it to the final arrows before Hu held on 146-144. Two points from gold, after two points to get there.
  • Ganesh Mani Ratnam Thirumuru, individual compound — SILVER for India. Fresh off the team final, he backed it up with a second gold medal match of the day — but ran into Wildman again, who shot the better arrows a second time, 148-146.

A big day for Australian archery, with Wildman's double gold the standout result of the whole stage. All four of India's team events reached the semi-finals in Hangzhou too, and two of them went all the way to gold medal matches — so did two individuals. Four medals from one stage, three of them silver by the narrowest of margins, is a genuinely strong week for India as well. Full results for every division are on World Archery's site.
