What is the Archery Asia Cup? Stage 3 under way in Hangzhou, two bronzes for India so far

Friday 20 November 2026

← All newsletters

Hi archers,

You might have seen "Asia Cup" mentioned around archery circles this year and wondered how it's different from the Hyundai Archery World Cup. It's World Archery Asia's own continental circuit: a series of World Ranking Tournaments run for and mostly by archers from Asian member federations, separate from World Archery's global World Cup series. The 2026 season has had three stages so far: Bangkok, Thailand in March; Sulaymaniyah, Iraq, which was postponed; and Hangzhou, China, which is under way right now.

STAGE 1: BANGKOK, THAILAND (MARCH)

Two bronzes confirmed for India, two more teams reached the final

On day two of Stage 1 in Bangkok, India's men's compound team (Rajat Chauhan, Rishabh Yadav and Uday Kamboj) topped qualification, sweeping the top four individual spots, but lost a tight semi-final 233-234 to Vietnam after squandering a five-point lead. They came back to beat Bhutan 234-232 in the bronze medal match. India's women's recurve team (Ruma Biswas, Kirti and Ridhi Phor) beat Malaysia 5-1 (sets 59-56, 53-50, 52-52) for bronze, having missed the podium at this venue the previous edition.

Two more Indian teams reached their finals against third-seeded Kazakhstan: the top-seeded men's recurve team (Devaang Gupta, Sukhchain Singh and Juyel Sarkar), who beat Malaysia 5-1 without dropping a set, and the women's compound team (Chikitha Taniparthi, Raj Kaur and Tejal Salve), who beat the Thai hosts 229-226 in a close semi-final. We don't have the result of either final, so we can't say yet whether those became gold or silver for India.

STAGE 2: SULAYMANIYAH, IRAQ — POSTPONED

Stage 2 was originally due in Sulaymaniyah, Iraq from 21 to 26 April, but World Archery pushed it back in light of the geopolitical situation in West Asia at the time, moving it to 25 to 30 October instead.

STAGE 3: HANGZHOU, CHINA — UNDER WAY

17 to 21 November, 168 archers from 17 nations, India fielding a 16-member squad

Stage 3 is on right now in Hangzhou, and India already has two more bronzes locked in, with two results still to be decided before the event closes. This is where things stood as of Friday 20 November, with finals still to come:

  • Women's compound team — BRONZE. Prithika Pradeep, Tejal and Aditi beat Australia 232-225 in the quarter-final, lost the semi-final 227-234 to South Korea, then beat Indonesia 230-228 in the bronze match.
  • Men's recurve team — BRONZE. Ujjwal, Goldi and Abhyuday beat Indonesia 5-3 in the quarter-final, lost the semi-final 0-6 to Australia, then won the bronze match in a shoot-off against Chinese Taipei, 5(30)-4(28).
  • Men's compound team — IN THE FINAL (not yet decided). Ganesh Mani Ratnam Thirumuru, Mohit and Manav beat Chinese Taipei 234-230 in the quarter-final and Singapore 231-224 in the semi-final. They play Australia for gold.
  • Women's recurve team — 4TH. Kirti, Yuktha Sri and Khushi beat North Korea 6-0 in the quarter-final, lost the semi-final to China 4(24)-5(27), then lost the bronze match 3-5 to South Korea.
  • Aditi Gopichand Swami, individual compound — IN THE GOLD MEDAL MATCH (not yet decided). The 2023 Berlin world champion beat South Korea's Oh Yoohyun 143-142 in the semi-final.

All four of India's team events reached the semi-finals in Hangzhou. We'll post the final results, including whether the men's compound team and Aditi Swami come away with gold or silver, once those matches are shot.
